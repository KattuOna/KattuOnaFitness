KattuOna Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 November 2026 · App: KattuOna (com.kattuona.app)

KattuOna is a personal fitness, nutrition, sleep and habit-tracking app with an optional AI coach. This page explains what the app stores and what, if anything, leaves your phone. In short: there is no KattuOna account and no sign-in; your logs live on your phone; they leave it only if you turn on a backup destination you pick yourself, or if you connect an AI feature to a server you choose. Nothing is sold, and there are no ads and no analytics.

1. What the app stores on your phone

  • Profile details you enter: name, birth year, sex, height, weight, body measurements, goals and units.
  • Your logs: workouts, meals and water, supplements (brand, product, serving size, ingredients and your own dose target/reminders), daily check-ins (sleep, soreness, energy), protocols and training programs.
  • Containers or utensils you calibrate for portion accuracy -- a name you give it (for example "my blue mug") and the water-weight you measured for it -- used only to work out the grams in a food you log by cup, glass, bowl or spoon. The "less full / level / heaping" choice on each logged food isn't stored on its own; it only affects the weight that gets saved for that one food log.
  • Settings, reminders, and your app passcode (stored only as a salted, one-way hash, never in readable form).
  • Any AI server address/access key or API key, and any backup server address/key you enter in Settings — these stay on this phone and are never included in any backup.

Uninstalling the app deletes all of this. There is no account to create and no login of any kind — the developer has no copy of this data and no way to access it. (Android's own device backup, if you have it switched on in Android's own settings, may copy app data to your Google account; that is controlled by Android and your Google account, not by KattuOna.)

2. Optional backup — a destination you choose

Settings → Your data lets you choose where backups go. Whichever you pick, the developer has no access to it:

  • Local only (default): nothing is uploaded anywhere. You can export a CSV copy yourself at any time, and KattuOna reminds you to keep that copy current.
  • Your own server: if you enter a server address (for example a NAS, a home server, or a small VPS you run), the app sends your backup directly to that address over HTTPS. That server is yours.
  • Your own Google Drive: you can sign in with Google and let KattuOna back up to a hidden, app-only folder in your own Drive (the "appdata" space) that only KattuOna can see — not the rest of your Drive. Only your Google account can read it.

3. The AI features (optional, off until you set one up)

KattuOna's AI coach, Chat with Kattu, food and exercise lookups, meal ideas, the readiness check and the program builder only work once you connect an AI engine in Settings → AI engine. There are two options, both configured by you, not the developer:

  • A self-hosted server: your own AI gateway in front of a model you run yourself (for example Ollama), or any compatible server you point the app at. The address, access key and model name you enter stay on your phone.
  • A direct API key: a base address, API key and model name for any provider whose API speaks the OpenAI chat-completions format (OpenAI, Groq, Together, Mistral, OpenRouter, DeepSeek, and others). Requests go straight from your phone to that provider, with no server in between.

Either way, a request can include what you typed and the profile/log details needed to answer it (for example age, sex, weight, recent calories, recent training or sleep). The developer does not operate either kind of AI engine and never sees these requests unless you've pointed the app at a server you yourself run. Responsibility for how a chosen server or provider handles what it receives rests with whoever operates it — read that provider's own privacy terms if it's a third party. AI answers can be wrong: they are general information, not medical advice.

Adding a meal from a photo: if your configured AI engine or model can understand images, KattuOna offers a camera button on each meal so you can photograph a dish instead of typing it in. With your permission to use the camera (or, if you pick a picture instead, the one picture you choose), that single photo is resized and sent to the same AI server or provider you've already set up in Settings → AI engine, purely to identify the dish and estimate its calories and macros — the same place Section 3 already describes. KattuOna itself does not keep a copy of the photo: it isn't saved to your logs, your backup, or anywhere on the device once the AI has answered. Camera access is optional and only ever used when you tap that button; you can refuse or later revoke it in Android Settings without losing any other feature.

4. Health Connect (optional)

If you allow it, KattuOna reads your daily step count and sleep sessions from Health Connect. It never writes to Health Connect. Steps estimate your daily activity; sleep times feed your recovery check-in and bedtime reminders. This data is used only inside KattuOna, stays on your phone (it may be included if you turn on your own chosen backup destination), and is never used for advertising. You can withdraw access at any time in Health Connect or in KattuOna's Settings.

Advanced wearables (optional, separate permission): if you connect a continuous-sensing wearable (for example an Oura, WHOOP, Garmin, or Temple once it ships) that writes to Health Connect, KattuOna can separately ask to read skin temperature, heart-rate variability, VO2max, blood oxygen (SpO2), and respiratory rate from Health Connect. This is requested only when you turn it on in Settings, and is used only to detect that such a device is connected and to show a plain heart-rate-variability-vs-weekly-average and latest-VO2max recovery read inside KattuOna -- skin temperature itself is currently only used to detect that a capable device is connected, not yet turned into a reading. None of this is sent anywhere; it stays on your phone the same as steps and sleep above. You can withdraw this access at any time in Health Connect or in KattuOna's Settings.

5. Diabetes-aware features (optional, off until you turn it on)

During setup, KattuOna asks once whether you manage diabetes (type 1, type 2, or prediabetes). You can also say "Prefer not to say," and you can change your answer at any time in Body metrics. If you don't say "Yes," the app works exactly as described everywhere else in this policy, with nothing extra shown or collected. This answer is stored on your phone the same way as your other body-metrics data (Section 1), and is included in a backup only if you turn one on (Section 2).

If you say "Yes," a few extra, already-scoped things turn on, all local to your phone unless you've turned on a backup:

  • Net carbs and fiber: food items can carry a fiber value alongside the usual calories/protein/ carbs/fat, so the app can show net carbs (carbs minus fiber) next to a logged food or meal. Fiber values you type in yourself, or that KattuOna's configured AI estimates (Section 3), are stored and shared the same way the rest of a food's nutrition data already is.
  • Safety cautions: a note that exercise can lower blood sugar appears on workout screens, and a caution appears on the app's optional morning-routine timing settings about not delaying a meal or medication dose around that schedule. These are static text, not personalized advice, and store nothing new about you.
  • Blood glucose logging: you can log a glucose reading by hand (a number and an optional note you write yourself), or sync read-only from a connected CGM or glucometer app via Health Connect, the same permission model as Section 4 -- KattuOna only ever reads blood glucose from Health Connect, never writes to it, and that permission is requested only when you tap "Sync" on this feature. Readings are stored on your phone like any other log, and included in a backup only if you turn one on.

None of this is medical advice. KattuOna does not suggest medication, insulin dosing or timing, does not diagnose anything from your numbers, and does not interpret trends in your readings -- it shows you the numbers you logged or synced, and nothing more. Always follow your own care team's guidance.

6. Calendar (optional)

If you turn on holiday-aware Smart Wake, KattuOna reads your calendar to check whether a day is a holiday or marked as leave. It only checks event dates/titles for that purpose — nothing beyond "is this day a holiday" is kept, and nothing leaves your phone. You can withdraw calendar access at any time in Android Settings or in KattuOna's Settings.

7. Automatic sleep detection (optional)

If you switch on automatic sleep detection, KattuOna can sense when you fall asleep and wake up in either (or both) of two ways, depending on what you grant:

  • Usage access: KattuOna looks at when your phone was last used at night and first used in the morning to estimate your bedtime and wake time. Which apps you used is not stored or sent anywhere -- only those two times are kept, on your phone.
  • Android's Sleep API (requires the "Physical activity" permission): with your permission, KattuOna asks Google Play services on your own device for your sleep segments -- the times you fell asleep and woke, with a confidence score, and nearby motion/light readings Play services derives for that purpose. This stays entirely on your phone and is used the same way as the usage-access estimate above: to set your bedtime/wake reminders and recovery check-in. It is not sent anywhere, including in backups of your other logs.

Either source only ever produces bedtime/wake times (and, for the Sleep API, the confidence/motion/light figures behind them) kept on your phone. You can withdraw "Usage access" or "Physical activity" access at any time in Android Settings or in KattuOna's Settings.

8. Supplement tracking (optional)

KattuOna lets you log the supplements you actually take -- brand, product, serving size and the ingredients on the label -- the same way it logs food and workouts, plus an optional reminder time you set yourself for each one. This stays on your phone like any other log, and is included in a backup only if you turn one on (Section 2).

Adding a supplement from a description or its label photo: the same AI mechanism described in Section 3 applies here -- if your configured AI engine supports images, you can describe a supplement in text or photograph its label instead of typing it in by hand, and that text or photo is sent once to the AI server or provider you already configured, purely to pre-fill a form you still review before saving. KattuOna doesn't keep a copy of the photo.

Scanning a barcode: this one is different from everything else in this policy -- it doesn't go through your configured AI engine, and it isn't a destination you chose. Tapping "Scan a barcode" opens your phone's camera through Google Play Services (KattuOna never receives or stores that camera photo itself), and the scanned barcode number -- not a photo, not any of your personal information, just the product's barcode -- is sent to Open Food Facts, a free, public product database, so KattuOna can look up that product's name and nutrition facts. This is the one place in the app where the developer has built in a fixed third party rather than one you choose yourself; see Open Food Facts' own privacy policy, linked above, for how they handle that request.

9. Shop (optional)

The Shop screen shows supplements and gear that may be useful for training, with a link to buy each one. KattuOna doesn't sell anything itself and doesn't handle payments: tapping a product opens that product's page in your browser, on the seller's own site, the same as tapping any other link -- you finish checkout there, and KattuOna never sees your payment details. Some of these links are affiliate links, which may earn the developer a small commission at no extra cost to you; this doesn't change what KattuOna does with your data.

The product list itself -- names, prices, photos and links -- comes from a plain content file in KattuOna's own GitHub repository, fetched over HTTPS each time you open the Shop screen. Product photos may be loaded from whichever image address is listed there. Neither of these requests includes anything about you: it's the same request every KattuOna install makes, not one built from your logs or profile.

Personalize Shop (optional, off by default): Shop has a tune icon in its own top corner that lets you turn on personalization -- using logs already on your phone to decide which products show first (it only reorders the list; it never adds, hides, or removes a product). Four sources, each its own switch you control: food & nutrition logs, workout history, body goal, and recent soreness. Turning personalization on, or any of its four switches, never sends anything anywhere -- the reordering happens entirely on your phone, and you can turn any part of it off again at any time from that same tune icon. A "Featured" section at the top of Shop is chosen by the developer and is the same for everyone; it isn't affected by personalization.

Rank with your AI engine (optional, off by default): in that same tune icon, a separate switch lets Shop ask your own configured AI engine -- the one you set up in Settings → AI engine, not a KattuOna server -- to rank "Picked for today" instead of the plain on-device scoring above. It's a bigger step than the four switches above: turning it on sends whichever of those you've already allowed (your recent food/workout summary, stated goal, or soreness) to that AI engine, once a day, as plain text asking it to rank the product list -- never a photo, never your full logs, and never anything if you haven't set up an AI engine or haven't allowed any of the four sources it draws from. If the AI is unreachable or answers with something unusable, Shop just keeps the plain on-device order for that day. Like the switches above, it's entirely optional and reversible from the tune icon.

10. Anonymous, shared food and exercise database

When you log a food or workout that isn't already in the app's built-in list, the app may contribute that item's name and nutrition or exercise values to a shared, anonymous database (hosted on Google Firebase) so other users searching for the same item don't have to enter it from scratch. This only ever includes the food/workout name and its nutritional or exercise values — never your personal logs, your name, or anything that identifies you. It can't be traced back to you, and nothing you log privately is ever included.

11. Crash reports and app integrity

The app uses Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase App Check. Crashlytics sends a crash report (the error, device model, Android version and a random installation ID — not linked to your name or any account) so problems can be fixed; it never includes your logs or profile. App Check confirms requests to the shared database in Section 9 come from a genuine copy of the app, without needing a sign-in. See Google's own handling of this data at firebase.google.com/support/privacy.

12. What the developer never does

  • No advertising, no ad tracking, no advertising ID.
  • No analytics on what you do in the app.
  • Never sells your data, and never shares anything that identifies you with advertisers or third parties. (The one exception, by design: a barcode number you choose to scan is sent to Open Food Facts to look that product up -- see Section 7. It carries no personal information.)
  • Never has access to your logs, your AI server, or your backup destination.
  • No account or login of any kind to create, lose, or have breached.

14. Deleting your data

There is no KattuOna account, so there is nothing to delete on the developer's end — every copy of your data is somewhere you control (a step-by-step version of this section is also at delete-account.html):

  • On your phone: Settings → Delete & advanced lets you wipe specific history; uninstalling the app removes everything stored on the phone.
  • A backup you turned on: delete the file on your own server, or remove KattuOna's app-data folder from your Google Drive (Google Account → Manage third-party access) — the developer has no copy to delete.
  • Health Connect: remove KattuOna's access in Health Connect at any time.
  • Calendar: remove KattuOna's calendar access in Android Settings at any time.

15. Children

KattuOna is intended for users aged 14 and up, and the app enforces this at setup. It is not directed at children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect data from children under 13.
